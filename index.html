About

After working as a developer, I studied computer science and mathematics at the Technion and did my MSc with Prof. Aviv Tamar on flow-based generative models. At ISTA, as a predoc intern and then during one year of PhD studies, I worked with Prof. Alex Bronstein and Prof. Paul Schanda on steering and reusing pretrained models for proteins, and with Prof. Mikhail Lemeshko on neural wave functions for few-body physics.

Each project built on what was already known instead of learning it from scratch. Next, I want to study one question directly: how do we add something new to a pretrained model without breaking what it already knows? I plan to approach it through representation learning, latent reasoning and continual learning.

Current interests

Three things today’s models entangle

Meaning from phrasing

Representation learning

LLMs often answer the same question differently when it is rephrased or reordered, perhaps because their representations tie what is meant to how it is said. Vision models like DINO and JEPA learn robust features by ignoring augmentations that preserve content and by predicting in latent space.

What are good augmentations for text, ones that change the phrasing but not the meaning?

Reasoning from language

Latent reasoning

Today's models mostly reason by writing out a chain of thought in tokens, so the reasoning is tied to the language it is written in. I'm interested in reasoning that happens in representation space, beyond explicit chain-of-thought.

How can we train reasoning without imitating human chain-of-thought, and how can we evaluate reasoning built on concepts humans don’t share?

Knowledge from skills

Modular continual learning

Large models learn facts and skills from the same data, all mixed together, so every update risks forgetting. I imagine universal skills living in the weights, domain knowledge in modules you add when needed, and specific facts (new, outdated or private) in an external memory.

Could science, which combines skills with domain knowledge and has testable answers, teach models to reuse skills in new domains?
